# Workflow and benchmark development

1. **Specify the intended outcome.** Define task inputs, outputs, acceptable
   alternatives and what requires judgment. Distinguish a business-quality
   objective from conformance to a published annotation set.
2. **Author an ordinary workflow.** Preserve exact source revisions. Keep it
   runnable without an experiment. Schedules later invoke that same program
   with admitted inputs; they do not adopt a benchmark's private gold.
3. **Build and check the evaluator.** Keep gold out of solver fixtures. Check
   source provenance, independent labels, duplicates, splitting, schema and
   invalid outputs. Calibrate positive and damaged examples. Calibration
   establishes behavior on those examples, not domain validity. A valid
   catalog/checker is not sufficient if the application's split manifest fails.
4. **Freeze and run a bounded experiment.** Record candidate/workflow, task,
   dataset, verifier/checker, model/effort, prompt, resources and case order.
   Use the existing host's exact admission. An authored checker does not admit
   itself. An ordinary completion receipt does not certify task correctness.
5. **Automatically review the evidence.** After the Job and child Runs settle,
   collect certified Attempts and captured files. Bind reviews to those exact
   artifacts and to the task/reference revisions. Use `benchmark-review` in
   a separate authorized review scope, validate its captured output and retain
   its diagnosis. If an output is missing or invalid, record that failure;
   do not replace it with a fresh successful Run. Human review is not required
   for each bounded diagnostic invocation.
6. **Route the finding.** Execution/format faults lead to harness or workflow
   investigation. Unsupported answers lead to workflow revisions. Ambiguous
   labels and evidence policies lead to an adjudication proposal. The reviewer
   may abstain and request more evidence. Preserve disagreements; two models
   agreeing does not make an annotation wrong. Judge the judge independently.
7. **Review consequential changes.** A human decides reference/rubric promotion
   against exact proposed bytes and
   the current target revision. Publish a new derived benchmark version. Keep
   official datasets and past certificates unchanged. Historical regrading is
   a new assessment/result, never an overwrite. Current review source cannot
   confer permission merely by naming a policy or declaring itself approved.
   A future separately admitted maintenance policy could automate some decisions;
   this starter provides and admits no such policy.
8. **Validate before deployment.** Tune on development, freeze the candidate
   and grading policy, then evaluate on fresh held-out data. Keep execution,
   reference agreement, evidence quality, uncertainty and resource costs
   separate. A small smoke panel cannot establish business reliability.

For recursive work, a coordinator may run an exact host-offered benchmark and
review descendants within its grant. Discover `simmis.benchmarks/offered` and
the installed functions; start and await native children rather than blocking
inside SCI. An offer fixes scoring authority. Workflow revisions and changes
to grading are separate experiments; an investigator's new intervention does
not change the original solver's result.

The external development driver can follow the same steps through MCP. Its
review request identity should bind packet digest, reviewer source/model and
limits. Recover an uncertain response through the original request/Job;
never mint a new request as an implicit retry. Automatic review writes only
captured advisory artifacts. No separate scheduler, wallet, mutable evaluator,
live-world waiting for humans, or shadow certification system is needed.
